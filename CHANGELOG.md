## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **TypeScript:** `ContractHandle.hardBudget(kind, explicitMs)` and `hardWarmup(mode, iterations)`, `anira::ContractHandle::hard_budget` / `hard_warmup`'s twins; `HardContractOptions.warmup`. A JavaScript processor's id (`anira_processor_desc.id`) is the key of the name it is defined under, so the handlers of pages that define the same processor share the model's figure. Tests: `web/test/warmup.test.ts`, the browser page `test/browser/warmup.html` (a warm-up before `ready()` is refused naming it; after it the pass reaches a JavaScript engine; a 1.5-hop-period engine on a pool of one prepares not real-time and renders offline ungated).
- **The model's figure (D4):** the core keeps what every warm-up pass measured, under either budget kind, keyed by the loaded model's record, a registered engine's id (two engine objects of one id share the figure, as processors of one id do; the 2.x catch-all `anira.v2.custom` excepted, whose figures stay per object) and the processor's inference hooks, for as long as its state lives. A later prepare of the model (another handler, a re-prepare at another block size or sample rate) reuses it and runs no pass (`ANIRA_WARMUP_OUTCOME_REUSED`), so identical plugins report identical latency; a prepare whose every plan reuses its figure sizes the session once and needs no running thread; two prepares of one model at once measure it once; a figure measured by a weaker warm-up (FIXED n) is measured again under a stronger one; `ANIRA_WARMUP_NONE` neither measures nor reads it. The prepare logs a reused figure with its age. **C ABI:** `anira_processor_desc.id`, a tail field: the identity of a processor's inference hooks, so that processors of one id share the figure (without one, a processor with `before_inference` or `after_inference` measures per registration); `anira::Processor(std::string id)` and `Processor::id()`. Tests: `AbiFigure.*` in `test/abi/test_Prepare.cpp`.
- **C ABI, handler: what the warm-up measured, per plan, and the real-time factor.** `anira_plan_info` gains the tail fields `warmup_iterations`, `warmup_outcome` (the new enum `anira_warmup_outcome`: `NONE`, `FIXED`, `STABLE`, `CAPPED`, `REUSED`), `measured_ms` and `median_ms` (the maximum and the median of the pass's last four inferences), `rt_factor` (the plan's inference time over the anchor's hop period, the latency model's kappa), `rt_parallel` (the session's parallel inferences) and `rt_verdict` (the new enum `anira_rt_verdict`: `UNMEASURED`, `COMFORTABLE` below a load of 0.75, `MARGINAL` below 1, `NOT_REALTIME`), an estimate at prepare; `ANIRA_PLAN_INFO_INIT` is extended. The prepare logs the measurement before the report, one record per plan and one for the session, at Warning for a marginal or not real-time plan, a pass that did not settle in 32 inferences, an explicit budget below its measurement and a pool that cannot keep up; the report's plan lines say "budget X ms (measured|explicit)". TypeScript: `PlanInfo.warmupIterations`, `warmupOutcome`, `measuredMs`, `medianMs`, `rtFactor`, `rtParallel`, `rtVerdict`, and the two enums. Tests: the `AbiWarmup.*` rows and records, `web/test/plan-report.test.ts` (the wasm32 offsets).
- **The MEASURED budget and the UNTIL_STABLE warm-up (PR 4):** `anira_handler_prepare` runs the contract's warm-up pass, ordinary inferences of every plan through the handler's session on the inference threads, each timed on its thread (the inference thread records every job's time, the hooks and the engine call, never the wait for a thread). The warm-up pass also measures the inference time; a measured budget is the maximum of its last four inferences; under an explicit budget the timings are only reported. UNTIL_STABLE (the default) stops at the first inference whose last four times lie within 20 % of their median or one clock step, at most 32; FIXED n runs n per plan. Under a MEASURED budget each plan's `anira_plan_info.budget_ms` is its figure and the session is sized for the slowest plan's; the session is sized again after the pass (the stream starts over, nothing of the pass reaches it or a declared State pair), and when that moves the entry count the registered engines and the processor are unprepared and prepared again with the final record (the first sizing takes the smallest figure a pass can measure above the sizing's tolerance for an instant inference, one clock step or more, so a fast model usually keeps its entry count). A prepare that warms up needs an inference thread inside its loop (`ANIRA_ERROR_INVALID_STATE` otherwise, or for an inference not complete within 10 s); an inference of the pass that fails fails the prepare with `ANIRA_ERROR_ENGINE`. Tests: `test/scheduler/test_Warmup.cpp` (the estimator), `AbiWarmup.*` in `test/abi/test_Prepare.cpp`, `AbiState.TheWarmupDoesNotLeakIntoTheStateOfARegisteredEngine`.
- **Model tensors of any dtype anira names:** `ANIRA_DTYPE_F32`, `F64`, `F16`, `BF16`, `I8`, `U8`, `I16`, `I32`, `I64` and `BOOL8`, for every role. The validator no longer refuses a model tensor that is not float32: each tensor travels in its spec's dtype end to end (its ring, the chunk's buffer, its Static store, its State pair), so a registered engine's descriptors carry each tensor's dtype on every call, as its load and prepare records already did. A dtype anira does not name (several lanes, an unsigned integer wider than 8 bits) is `ANIRA_ERROR_NOT_SUPPORTED` at `anira_handler_create`. Nothing converts: a tensor's ring streams its spec's dtype unless the contract names another, and a host of another sample type names its type in `ring_dtypes` and adds a processor that converts in `pre_process` / `post_process` with its own scaling. Tests: `test/abi/test_Dtypes.cpp` (a model of each of the ten dtypes through a registered engine with no ring setting, a typed Static tensor, the per-call refusal of a host tensor of another dtype, float16 and int8 models behind a float32 host's converting processor).
- **C ABI, handler:** the plan report names each slot's dtypes: `anira_plan_slot` gains the tail fields `dtype` (the spec's, the model tensor's) and `ring_dtype` (the ring's, the host end of a Streamed tensor; 0 for a slot without a ring), the prepare log's slot lines name both, and the TypeScript `PlanSlot` mirrors them (`dtype`, `ringDtype`).
- **C ABI, handler:** the plan report names the window each slot runs at: `anira_plan_slot` gains the tail fields `window_derived` (1 where the spec declared no window and the validator derived it, Changed), `window` (the window in elements along the Time axis per inference, a flexible one as the anchor hop pinned it) and `hop` (its advance per inference, the window less the overlap), 0 for all three on a slot without a window (Static, State); the same in every plan. The prepare log's slot lines name them: "window 15380, hop 2048", "window 512 (derived from the Time extent), hop 512", "window 2048 (derived, flexible, pinned to the anchor hop), hop 2048", "window 2048 (flexible, pinned to the anchor hop), hop 2048". TypeScript: `PlanSlot.windowDerived`, `window` and `hop`. Tests: `AbiPrepare.ThePlanReportShowsTheWindowInUseAndWhetherItWasDerived`, `web/test/plan-report.test.ts` (the wasm32 offsets), `test/browser/dtypes.html` (the rows of a model file whose output declares no window, read from the module).
- **C ABI, handler and context:** who moves an edge's bytes, beside where they go. The new enum `anira_mover_flags` names the movers as bits:
  - `ANIRA_MOVER_ANIRA` (1): anira's own code, an engine adapter's copy;
  - `ANIRA_MOVER_ENGINE` (2): the runtime's code, a copy its API makes, a device provider's upload and readback, a registered engine's code;
  - `ANIRA_MOVER_NONE` (0) where nothing moves.

  The new field `moved_by`, a tail field of `anira_plan_slot` and a field of the two-ended `anira_edge_info` (Changed), holds an OR of them, with both bits where anira and the runtime each move the bytes (a memory-planned ExecuTorch output on a device delegate), the edge's class then the worse move's. The rows with the `ANIRA_MOVER_ANIRA` bit are exactly the copies anira makes: per inference, the plan's executors count exactly them, on every built-in engine of the build (`AbiDtypes.TheTypedGainFixturesRunOnEveryTypedEngine`, `TheTypedGainFixturesRunOnALiteRtAccelerator`, `TheUnplannedExecuTorchFixturesAreBoundInPlace`). The prepare log's slot lines say "moved by none | anira | the engine | anira and the engine". TypeScript: `PlanSlot.movedBy`.
- **C ABI, handler and context:** the memory kind of a provider's device, the fact a device provider's edge class follows (Changed). The new enum `anira_memory_kind`:
  - `ANIRA_MEMORY_UNKNOWN` (0): no probe established it (the device's API is missing or failed, the provider maps to no API anira can ask, the adapters a self-choosing runtime picks among disagree), where a device's crossing is `ANIRA_EDGE_UNKNOWN`, which every limit of the edge cost refuses;
  - `ANIRA_MEMORY_UNIFIED` (1): the host's own physical memory (the CPU path, an integrated GPU, Apple silicon, a CPU device such as Mesa's lavapipe), where a device's crossing is `ANIRA_EDGE_UNIFIED_TRANSFER`;
  - `ANIRA_MEMORY_DEDICATED` (2): memory of the device's own across a bus (a discrete GPU's VRAM), where a crossing is `ANIRA_EDGE_DEDICATED_TRANSFER`.

  The values share the words of the two transfer classes.

  The new field `memory_kind` of the two-ended `anira_edge_info` (the capability rows, Changed) and tail field of `anira_plan_info` (the plan rows) reports it, `UNIFIED` on a provider that runs over host memory; the prepare log's plan lines end in "memory unified | dedicated | unknown"; TypeScript: `PlanInfo.memoryKind`. anira asks the device's own API, each loaded at run time with no new link dependency (`src/platform/memory_probe.cpp`):
  - Metal's `hasUnifiedMemory` of the system default device (Apple builds);
  - the Vulkan device type, cross-checked with its memory: an integrated GPU or a CPU device is unified when a memory type is both device-local and host-visible, a discrete GPU dedicated when a heap is not device-local;
  - the CUDA driver's `CU_DEVICE_ATTRIBUTE_INTEGRATED`;
  - HIP's `hipDeviceAttributeIntegrated` (HIP 5.0 and later);
  - OpenCL's `CL_DEVICE_HOST_UNIFIED_MEMORY`;
  - D3D12's `D3D12_FEATURE_DATA_ARCHITECTURE.UMA` per DXGI adapter;
  - on the web nothing: a browser exposes no memory kind, so every device provider is unknown there.

  A provider names its device: CUDA and ONNX Runtime's TensorRT the CUDA device of ONNX Runtime's `device_id` option (0 without one), ROCm and MIGraphX the HIP device of it, DirectML the DXGI adapter of it, Core ML and ExecuTorch's MPSBackend the Metal default device; WebGPU through Dawn, LiteRT's `gpu` accelerator and Vulkan every device of the API the runtime picks among (Metal on Apple, D3D12 on Windows, Vulkan elsewhere, OpenCL for LiteRT on Android), their kind when all agree, else unknown naming each; an NPU and any other custom provider unknown. The probe is lazy: the first read of a device provider's capability row or a prepare with a plan on it asks, never the context's creation and never the audio thread. Each API is asked once per context on a thread of its own, waited for 5 s at most, and kept until `anira_context_probe`; each answer is a Debug record of `anira.system`. On the development box: CUDA and OpenCL dedicated on an NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000, Vulkan unified on lavapipe. Tests: the new binary `test_platform` (`MemoryProbe`: every API answers consistently, the Vulkan cross-check, a probe that hangs answers its timeout; `ANIRA_TEST_MEMORY_KINDS="CUDA=dedicated;OpenCL=dedicated;Vulkan=unified"` asserts a known machine's devices), `AbiMemoryKind` (every provider's device and the rule per kind, on fake machines), `AbiEngine.TheClassOfADeviceProviderFollowsItsDevicesMemory`, `AbiEngine.TheMemoryKindIsAskedLazilyAndKeptByTheContext`.
- **C ABI, engines:** a registered engine states how it passes each slot to its runtime, for the plan report and the contract's edge cost, as a built-in engine's adapter does:
  - the new record `anira_engine_edge` (`edge_class`, `reason`) and callback `anira_engine_edge_fn` in the new tail field `anira_engine_desc.edge`;
  - it is asked once per slot right after each load, inputs first, the record arriving with the provider rule's edge (the class of the load's device memory kind on a device provider), and the engine overwrites what differs;
  - the mover follows from the class: the engine's code moves whatever moves;
  - NULL keeps the provider rule;
  - a refused statement fails the prepare with its status, a class the enum does not name with `ANIRA_ERROR_ENGINE`.

  `anira.hpp`: `anira::EngineEdge` and the virtual `Engine::Loaded::edge(is_input, slot, provider_rule)`, whose base returns the rule. Tests: `AbiEngine.ARegisteredEngineStatesItsEdges`, `AbiCxx.AnEngineStatesItsEdges`.
- **Web:** `HardContractOptions.edgeCost` and `ContractHandle.edgeCost` (`anira::Hard::edge_cost`, `anira::ContractHandle::edge_cost`) set a contract's edge cost; left out, or `edgeCost(undefined)`, there is no limit. `EngineOptions.edge` (the type `EngineEdges`, `{ inputs?: EngineEdge; outputs?: EngineEdge }`) is a JavaScript engine's edge statements per direction, one for every input slot and one for every output slot, given on the main thread at `Pipeline.addEngine`, since the engine runs in the inference Workers; a direction left out keeps the provider rule of that direction. Per direction where C++'s `Engine::Loaded::edge(is_input, slot, …)` is per slot: `addEngine` runs before the model is known. A statement for both directions is `edge: { inputs: X, outputs: X }`. The engines the package ships state theirs by default under their id: `addEngine(ONNXRUNTIME_WEB_ENGINE_ID)` states the ONNX Runtime Web engine's, `ANIRA_EDGE_COPY` moved by the engine both ways, since anira's module and onnxruntime-web's are two wasm memories ("onnxruntime-web: copied into ORT's heap" for the inputs, "… copied out of ORT's heap" for the outputs); `ONNXRUNTIME_WEB_ENGINE_EDGES` (root, scope and engine entries) are the statements, for the engine defined under another id, and a given `edge` replaces them whole. `test/pipeline.test.ts` checks the records per direction (the default, a statement for one direction, an override, another id), `test/browser/dtypes.html` both engines' rows and each side's reason.
- **Bundled models:** TypedGainNetwork over example-models `28e6dd9` (`extras/fetch-models.cmake`'s pin, which moves from `d1bc5cf`): the gain fixture's tensors at `int16`, `int8` and `float16`, computed in float32 and cast at the boundary, with a row for every engine (`extras/models/model-pool/typed_gain_<dtype>.model.json`) and an ExecuTorch export at a static 512-sample window whose inputs and outputs the memory plan leaves to the caller (`typed_gain_<dtype>_unplanned.model.json`); an ONNX model per dtype anira names (`dtype_matrix_<dtype>.onnx`) and a fully integer TFLite model whose int8 I/O carries a scale and a zero point (`quantized_gain_network_int8.tflite`), named in `extras/models/model_files.h`. ### Changed

- **Breaking:** the bundled contract files (`extras/models/**/*.contract.json`: the CNN, HybridNN, stateful RNN, gain, stereo gain, stateful accumulator and the three RAVE files) run the library's defaults, `"budget": "measured", "warmup": "until_stable"` (the RAVE encoder and decoder keep `"on_miss": "zeros"`), so the latency they give depends on the machine; the examples and benchmarks that load them run measured budgets. Migration: pin `"budget": {"ms": x}` (with `"warmup": "none"` or `{"fixed": n}`) for a reproducible latency, as `anira::ContractHandle::hard_budget` / `hard_warmup` do over a loaded file. The test suites pin the files' former figures from one table (`test/support/file_contracts.h`), so every latency they assert stays.
- **Examples:** the JUCE and CLAP plugins, the benchmark fixture's run header, the C install consumer and both web examples print what prepare measured, per plan: the budget, the warm-up's iterations and outcome, the real-time factor and verdict. A backend whose plan is not real-time on this machine is held until the user agrees: the JUCE plugin runs its bypass plan (the audio passes through, time-aligned) and its new editor (`PluginEditor`, the generic controls plus the verdict line) shows a "Try nonetheless" button; the CLAP plugin runs its Bypassed plan until its new "Try nonetheless" parameter is on, and implements `clap.render`; the web examples hold their audio node (`AniraAudioNode.hold`) on a live context until their page's "Try nonetheless" button, and their mount functions take the button and the verdict line. An offline render is never held (JUCE `isNonRealtime()`, CLAP `CLAP_RENDER_OFFLINE`, the web's `waitMs > 0`), a benchmark measures whatever the verdict, and an accepted verdict asks again only when a re-prepare finds a worse one. The C install consumer (`test/contracts/install/main_c.c`) prepares on the defaults (no budget or warm-up setter) and exits 0 whatever the verdict.
- **Docs:** the design document (`docs/anira-v3-architecture.md`) describes the measured budget as built: the contract section (3) states the defaults, the warm-up pass, the plan rows' real-time verdict and the model's figure, and that a Hard prepare never returns `ANIRA_ERROR_BUDGET` (kept for Async deadlines); the usage sketches (9) prepare on the defaults, the C one without the budget and warm-up setters as `consumer_c` runs it; the plan row (6), the contract file's encodings (8.3) and the M2b roadmap's PR 4 item (11) no longer date the refusal of the defaults.
- **Breaking (web):** the pool's Workers start at `Context.create` (`anira_context_create`) and live while a context or a handler's session does, so a re-prepare keeps its booted Workers; a prepare whose contract warms up runs its pass on them and needs `await context.ready()` first (`ANIRA_ERROR_INVALID_STATE` naming it otherwise). `Context.ready()` on a context without a pool now waits for one of the page's threads in its loop. Migration: `await context.ready()` before the prepare, or an explicit budget with `warmup: ANIRA_WARMUP_NONE`.
- **Breaking (TypeScript):** `ContractHandle.hard` mirrors `anira::Hard`: `budgetMs` is optional (left out, the measured budget) and the warm-up defaults to `UNTIL_STABLE` (it was NONE unless `warmupIterations` was set). Migration: `budgetMs` plus `warmup: ANIRA_WARMUP_NONE` for the former explicit budget without a pass, `warmup: ANIRA_WARMUP_FIXED` beside `warmupIterations` for a fixed one. The browser pages that prepare before their Workers run are pinned so; `config.html` awaits `ready()` and reads the rows' warm-up fields; the two web examples await `ready()` before their prepare, and `processor-and-engine` runs the defaults.
- `anira::Budget` default-constructs to the measured budget, as a fresh C contract does, so `anira::Hard{.host_block_min = 512, .host_block_max = 512, .host_rate = 48000}` prepares with the library's defaults (the budget the warm-up pass measures, UNTIL_STABLE); a duration is an explicit budget as before. Not Breaking: every aggregate that named its budget keeps it. The install consumer prepares with these defaults. Tests: `AbiCxx.AHardWithItsHostBlocksAlonePreparesWithTheDefaults`.
- The latency calculator logs nothing: the "inference pool cannot keep up" Warning moved into the handler's prepare, once per prepare after the session's final sizing (the session's record), beside the plans' verdicts.
- **Breaking:** the contract's FIXED warm-up runs per plan at every prepare, through the session, instead of per executor when the model loads; `0` is still none. Every executor of a built-in engine runs one inference over its own scratch when it is made (at load, or at an exclusive handler's prepare), and the pool no longer keys a loaded model on the warm-up count: two handlers that differ only in it share their model. Migration: none for a contract; a test that counted engine or hook calls at prepare counts the pass's now.
- **Breaking:** a MEASURED budget with `ANIRA_WARMUP_NONE` or FIXED 0 is `ANIRA_ERROR_CONFIG` at prepare naming the warm-up (it was `ANIRA_ERROR_NOT_SUPPORTED`, as was every MEASURED budget); an explicit budget takes every warm-up, UNTIL_STABLE included.
- A model too slow for the stream prepares under a MEASURED budget as under an explicit one the pool cannot keep up with (the session sized for one host block's burst, the latency calculator's Warning); a Hard prepare never returns `ANIRA_ERROR_BUDGET`, which the registry now reserves for the Async contract's deadlines.
- Natively `anira_inference_thread_start` returns once its thread is inside its loop, as a pool's start already did, so a prepare that warms up right after it finds the thread serving (WebAssembly: the Worker still enters its loop after the call).
- **Web:** the module's memory starts at 128 MiB by default and at 64 MiB at least, where both were 256 MiB (`Anira.create`'s default and least `initial`, and AniraWeb's `-sINITIAL_MEMORY`, 64 MiB). The 256 MiB held the 169 MiB the core's inference queue preallocated (Fixed); with the fix the heap ends at 34 MiB after `Anira.create` (the module's data and 32 MiB main stack), at 42 MiB with one Worker and a handler of the bundled gain model, and at 103 MiB with the model-file example's 16 Workers on a machine of 32 hardware threads. 64 MiB holds the core with a first Worker and handler, 128 MiB the model-file example with about a quarter to spare; the memory grows from there as before. Not breaking: every size accepted before still is. Docs: `web-api/installation_building.rst` (the memory: what it holds at each step; for sizing, #207: a handler's memory from its chunk pool, rings, State pairs and queue room, with the gain model as a worked example, and what each engine keeps of the model, ONNX Runtime a copy of the weights per executor; #207's figures before), `basic_usage.rst`, `architecture.rst`. Test: `test/browser/memory.html` (a context of two Workers and a handler fit the default memory without a growth, under 48 MiB past the heap after `Anira.create`); `growth.html` refuses 32 MiB, starts at 128 MiB, and grows the memory with blocks of the main thread's malloc where it created handlers (2,000 handlers no longer grow it: a handler of the gain model's contract takes about 40 KiB beside its model).
- **Model config:** a Streamed tensor's window is optional. A spec that declares none (no `anira_tensor_spec_set_window` call or one with (0, 0), `TensorSpec::window` never called, no `"window"` key in a model file) has it derived when the configuration is validated, where it was refused with `ANIRA_ERROR_CONFIG` ("window_min must be positive"): the extent of a fixed Time axis is the window (overlap 0), so a model whose shape already says it needs no window; a dynamic Time axis makes it flexible, `[1, ANIRA_UNBOUNDED]`, pinned with the anchor hop like a declared flexible window (#198). A declared window always wins, so every configuration accepted before keeps its windows. An overlap without a window is not derived: `ANIRA_ERROR_CONFIG` naming the tensor and asking for the window. The derivation never writes into the spec or the model config: `anira_tensor_spec_window` (`SpecView::window`) still reads (0, 0, 0), and `anira_model_config_to_json` writes a window back exactly when one was declared (an overlap without one as it was read); the window in use is the plan report's (Added) and the prepare log's. Docs: `anira_tensor_spec_set_window`, `anira_tensor_spec_window`, `TensorSpec::window`, `usage.rst` sections 1.1, 1.3, 1.5 and 3.2. Tests: `AbiValidate.AnOmittedWindowIsDerivedFromTheTimeAxis` (a fixed extent, a dynamic axis pinned at two host blocks and without any, beside a fixed anchor, a declared window winning, a refusal naming the derived window), `AbiValidate.WindowRules` (no window derived, an overlap without one refused, `(0, 512)` still refused), `AbiJsonModel.AWindowIsWrittenBackExactlyWhenDeclared`.
- **Breaking:** `anira_edge_class` says only where an edge takes the bytes: four classes, each its own sort of move, ordered by cost so a chain's worst edge is its largest value (the cost scale), and one class outside it:
  - `ANIRA_EDGE_ZERO_COPY` = 0: nothing moves, the engine computes on the slot's own memory (a device on unified memory included, where its runtime wraps the buffer);
  - the new `ANIRA_EDGE_COPY` = 1: a copy or pass within one domain, the CPU's or one device's, with no hand-over between processors (a memcpy in host memory, a relayout or copy inside a device's memory);
  - the new `ANIRA_EDGE_UNIFIED_TRANSFER` = 2: a crossing between the CPU's domain and a device's within one unified physical memory (Apple silicon, an integrated GPU, a CPU device), a copy plus a hand-over between the processors (a submission, a fence, possibly cache maintenance or a relayout) and no bus;
  - the new `ANIRA_EDGE_DEDICATED_TRANSFER` = 3: a crossing over a bus to or from dedicated memory (a DMA to or from a discrete GPU's VRAM, a discrete device reading mapped pinned host memory, a copy between two devices);

## [v2.3.0] - 2026-09-02

anira v2.3.0 is the last release of the 2.x line. Development continues on the v3 branch (the versioned C ABI, `docs/anira-v3-architecture.md`); 2.x receives fixes only.

### Added

- One-sided streaming (#98, #99, #110): generators are driven by output demand, analysers read through `PrePostProcessor::get_output()`, and the `HostConfig` reference stream may be an input or an output (`m_tensor_is_input`, `HostConfig::k_first_streamable`). - `LatencyCalculator` (#66) replaces `SessionElement`'s latency arithmetic: closed forms instead of the LCM loop, `is_feasible()`, correct struct and ring sizing for fractional and smaller host blocks. Derivation in `docs/sphinx/latency.rst`.

Migration: link `anira::<engine>` wherever your own code includes an engine header.
- **Breaking** (Windows install layout): DLLs install to `bin/`, import libraries and archives to `lib/`.
- **Breaking:** logging goes through tanh-lib's `thl::Logger` with a real-time queue (#56): `ANIRA_LOG_*`/`ANIRA_LOG_RT_*` replace the `LOG_*` stream macros, `ContextConfig::m_log_level` -> `ContextConfig::m_log` (`anira::LogConfig`), and the host owns the sinks.
- **Breaking:** `InferenceHandler::reset()` is wait-free and safe on the audio thread. Migration: call `prepare()` where a drain was relied upon.
- **Breaking** (containers): `anira::Buffer<T>`, `anira::RingBuffer` and `anira::MemoryBlock<T>` are aliases over tanh-lib's `thl::core` containers; `RingBuffer` no longer derives from `Buffer<float>`, and every real-time ring-buffer access uses the block API (#111).
- `Context::get_instance()` takes no arguments and returns a `Context&`; the `ContextConfig` travels with `Context::create_session()` (old two-step API deprecated for one minor release). `anira::HighPriorityThread` is deprecated for one release.
- The active backend defaults to the first model backend that is available instead of the silent `CUSTOM` bypass; `HostConfig`'s default reference tensor is the first streamable tensor.
- The export header is `anira/system/Exports.h`; `ANIRA_BUILDING`/`ANIRA_STATIC` replace `ANIRA_EXPORTS`/`ANIRA_STATIC_DEFINE` (old spellings honoured).
- ExecuTorch is consumed as one merged `libexecutorch.a` per platform, and the CMake >= 3.24 desktop floor is gone. The test suite builds as per-component binaries (`test_utils`, `test_scheduler`, `test_backends`, `test_handler`).
- Pins: tanh-lib `751b2b1`, anira-project/backends v2.4.0 (ExecuTorch 1.3.1, pre-isolated static desktop LiteRT).

### Removed

- `Context::release_instance()` and `Context::release_thread_pool()` (`Context::shutdown()` is the explicit teardown), the never-released `reset_non_blocking()` variants, the unreachable `ContextConfig::operator==`/`!=`, and the dead interpolation branch of `anira::calculate_percentile`.

### Fixed

- Backend runtime symbols are no longer exported from binaries embedding anira, which crashed hosts that ship their own runtime (Ableton Live 12 bundles ONNX Runtime).
- One-sided streaming: `SessionElement::prepare()` no longer hangs for generators or crashes for analysers, push-only pipelines no longer stall after `m_num_structs` chunks (#99), latency vectors are index-aligned with the outputs (#98).
- Session lifecycle: `Context::create_session()` no longer leaks the session count when a processor constructor or `prepare()` throws (#106); `create_session()`/`release_session()`/`prepare_session()` are thread-safe across sessions; a data race in `Context::release_session()` (found by the TSan leg).
- `LatencyCalculator::least_common_multiple` no longer overflows `int`, the `allow_smaller_buffers` sweep no longer stalls for large blocks, and the send ring of a fractional host block reserves `P - 1` samples.
- `JsonConfigLoader` drops malformed `tensor_shape` entries instead of letting `nlohmann::type_error` escape; `LibtorchProcessor` throws `std::runtime_error` on an unloadable model like the other backends; `BackendBase::process` no longer reads past the output vector.
- `USE_ANIRA_WEB` is defined in Emscripten builds only; installing on `lib64` hosts works with LibTorch and ExecuTorch; `anira::HighPriorityThread` links again in a Windows consumer of a shared anira; the JUCE example links under clang on Linux.
- Tests and CI: the macOS test-discovery race with CMake 4.4.0 (`DISCOVERY_MODE PRE_TEST`), the intermittently failing one-sided blocking-deadline tests, downloads retried after transient failures, and compile caching that works for the first time (`ACTIONS_CACHE_SERVICE_V2`).

## [v2.2.1] - 2026-07-04

### Added

- Batched overlapping-window extraction: a `pop_samples_from_buffer(...)` overload that extracts `num_batches` windows in one native call (also exposed to WebAssembly/TS), avoiding per-batch JS↔Wasm boundary crossings for batched/windowed models (e.g. HybridNN/GuitarLSTM).
- `PrePostProcessor::before_inference()` / `after_inference()` hooks, run on the inference thread right before/after the backend runs, letting stateful models (e.g. recurrent hidden-state feedback) splice state between consecutive inferences when combined with `session_exclusive_processor = true`. Wired up end-to-end in Anira Web (`registerPrePostProcessor`/`beforeInference()`/`afterInference()` on the TS side).
- Configurable inference-thread wait strategy: `anira::WaitStrategy { SpinBackoff, Blocking }` on `ContextConfig`. `Blocking` lets idle inference threads sleep on a semaphore instead of spin-polling, eliminating idle CPU usage at effectively no throughput cost; coerced to `SpinBackoff` with a warning on WebAssembly.
- Real-time factor and underrun reporting in the benchmark fixture (per-iteration RTF/`[underrun]` marker, plus an optional summary line with `rtf_mean`/`rtf_max`/underrun count).
- The `advanced` and `cnn-size` benchmarks now also cover the `CUSTOM` (no-inference) backend, and the benchmark CI workflow builds with tests enabled and runs the benchmark gtest suites.
- Configurable log level: `anira::LogLevel { Debug, Info, Warning, Error }` on `ContextConfig`, gating anira's own logging and forwarded to the ONNX Runtime, LiteRT and LibTorch backends (TFLite excepted — no runtime logging control in that prebuilt library). Defaults to `Info` in debug builds, `Error` in release builds.
- Anira version mismatch reporting in `Context::get_instance`: a differing major version between sessions is now reported as an error, a differing minor/patch as a warning.
- `Context::get_num_inference_threads()` / `InferenceHandler::get_num_inference_threads()`: number of inference threads currently active process-wide (also exported to WASM).

### Changed

- `ContextConfig`'s default `num_threads` is now platform-dependent: half the available CPU cores (as before) on native builds, `0` on WebAssembly, since inference threads there are always supplied externally via `AniraWeb.spinUpInferenceWorker()`.

### Fixed

- Stateful in-order dispatch (`session_exclusive_processor = true`) no longer allocates on the audio thread — `SessionElement`'s pending-dispatch queue is now pre-sized and fed through an explicit producer token.
- Web: the audio worklet now polyfills `performance.now()` before the WASM module loads, fixing a `ReferenceError` (observed in Firefox) whenever a blocking-ratio deadline was read from the audio thread.
- The advanced benchmark no longer instantiates ONNX Runtime for the stateful RNN model, whose fixed 2048-sample ONNX export failed warm-up at other buffer sizes.
- Use-after-free when a pooled backend processor outlived the session that created it — `InferenceConfig` is now owned per-processor instead of aliasing the originating session's copy.
- Windows build failure in `Context::get_instance()` caused by `<windows.h>`'s `min`/`max` macros mangling an unrelated `std::min(...)` call; fixed by defining `NOMINMAX`/`WIN32_LEAN_AND_MEAN` before the include.
- Hardened `SessionElement::m_is_non_real_time` to `std::atomic<bool>` and unified the two `new_data_request()` wait paths onto one `wait_for_completion()` helper — previously a default-`blocking_ratio` session in non-real-time mode could free an in-flight buffer while the inference thread was still writing to it. Non-real-time mode also now works on WebAssembly once an inference worker is running.

## [v2.2.0] - 2026-06-23

### Added

- **Android support** (`arm64-v8a` + `x86_64`): the library, the prebuilt backends, and the full gtest suite running on a KVM-accelerated emulator, all wired into CI (`build_test_mobile.yml`). The glibc-only `pthread_*inheritsched` / `pthread_setattr_default_np` calls are gated behind `!__ANDROID__` (bionic lacks them) while keeping the portable `SCHED_FIFO` path.
- **iOS support** (device + simulator): the library and prebuilt backends shipped as an xcframework, with the full gtest suite running on the simulator in CI. Per-SDK xcframework slice selection; TFLite is consumed as a `TensorFlowLiteC.framework` via a generated shim so anira's `<tensorflow/lite/...>` includes resolve untouched.
- **LiteRT inference backend** (`anira::InferenceBackend::LITERT`): runs `.tflite` models through Google's native `LiteRt*` C API / CompiledModel runtime. Enabled by default (`ANIRA_WITH_LITERT=ON`) as the modern TensorFlow-Lite-family backend; wired into the examples and benchmarks.
- Data-driven backend downloads: prebuilt backends are fetched at configure time from the [`anira-project/backends`](https://github.com/anira-project/backends) release, pinned by `ANIRA_BACKENDS_VERSION`.
- Live backend integrity check: when GitHub is reachable, anira fetches each asset's published SHA256 and re-downloads any backend whose archive changed upstream or downloaded incompletely, instead of a committed hash lock. `ANIRA_BACKENDS_SKIP_REMOTE_CHECK=ON` skips the remote query for offline/reproducible builds.
- Bring-your-own-backend knobs: `ANIRA_<ENGINE>_ROOTDIR` (prebuilt tree), `ANIRA_<ENGINE>_URL` + `ANIRA_<ENGINE>_SHA256` (custom source), and per-engine `ANIRA_<ENGINE>_LINKAGE=shared|static`.
- clang-tidy conformance across the library, tests and benchmark sources, enforced in CI via the `tanh-lab/ci-actions/clang-tidy-check` action (`clang_tidy.yml`)
- `InstallConsumer` smoke test that builds against the installed package to catch packaging regressions.
- Release pipeline now publishes every tested arch×linkage artifact with backend-consistent naming: Android (static), iOS (xcframework), and Linux/macOS/Windows × `shared`/`static`, including macOS `universal`.

### Changed

- **Breaking:** the `InferenceConfig::Defaults` compile-time constants were renamed from the `m_` prefix to the `k_` prefix to match the constant-naming convention (`m_warm_up` → `k_warm_up`, `m_session_exclusive_processor` → `k_session_exclusive_processor`, `m_blocking_ratio` → `k_blocking_ratio`). The mutable `Defaults::m_num_parallel_processors` is unchanged.
- LiteRT is now the default TensorFlow-Lite-family backend. The legacy TensorFlow Lite backend (the older `TfLite*` C API, `ANIRA_WITH_TFLITE`) is the **same runtime** exposed through a different C API, so the two are now **mutually exclusive** — enable the legacy path with `-DANIRA_WITH_LITERT=OFF -DANIRA_WITH_TFLITE=ON`.
- `nlohmann_json` is now consumed as a release download instead of a git submodule.
- CMake options and their validation were consolidated inline into `CMakeLists.txt` (`AniraOptions.cmake` and the redundant linkage knob were dropped).
- `anira::calculate_min` / `anira::calculate_max` are now `inline` free functions instead of `const auto` lambdas (source-compatible: existing call sites and uses as a callable are unaffected)
- The internal logging helper `isLoggingEnabled()` was renamed to `is_logging_enabled()`
- Migrated the shared clang configs (`.clang-format`/`.clang-tidy`/`.clangd`) from the `tanh-lib` submodule symlinks to [`tanh-tooling`](https://github.com/tanh-lab/tanh-tooling) (pinned `v0.1.4`): committed as real files, kept in sync by the `clang_check.yml` drift check, and the now-unused `tanh-lib` submodule was removed (configs are byte-identical, so lint/format results are unchanged)
- Adopted the default Claude Code config: `.claude/settings.json` now enables the `tanh-tools` plugin from the tanh-tooling marketplace (its format/lint/type-check hooks supersede the previous bespoke `.claude/hooks`)
- CI now covers Windows-`arm64`, Linux-`aarch64` and macOS-`universal` legs (shared + static) in addition to the mobile workflow.

### Fixed

- Potential use-after-free in `Buffer::malloc_channels()` when channel-pointer allocation fails
- Installed `nlohmann_json` config so `find_package(anira)` works against the installed package
- Disabled backends now compile to empty translation units (guarded `.cpp` bodies), and `minimal-onnxruntime` is self-sufficient in static builds
- Value-initialize the `const InferenceConfig` in the install consumer
- clang-tidy violations and a CLAP static MSVC runtime mismatch; preserve the anira-before-`JuceHeader` include order on MSVC; qualified `mem*` calls with `std::` and added `<cstring>`
- Windows shared-DLL copy, the examples target list, and arm64 LibTorch in CI
